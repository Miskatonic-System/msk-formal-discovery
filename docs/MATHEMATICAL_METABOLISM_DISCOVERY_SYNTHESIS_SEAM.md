# Mathematical Metabolism: Discovery and Synthesis Seam

**Status:** `PARKED_PROVIDER_SEAM`  
**Authority:** `NONE`

## Purpose

Record the future role of `msk-formal-discovery` inside the Mathematical Metabolism lifecycle without promoting search output, abstraction candidates, or refactoring proposals into theorem authority.

`miskatonic-mathematics` owns the lifecycle semantics and canonical mathematical interpretation. `msk-formal-discovery` remains a non-authoritative provider for discovery, decomposition, abstraction mining, candidate recombination, and prospective usefulness measurements.

## Supported future roles

The engine may eventually contribute candidate artifacts to these lifecycle stages:

```text
DISCOVERY / GENERATION
        |
        +--> candidate proofs / lemmas / decompositions
        +--> repeated-structure mining
        +--> candidate abstractions
        |
        v
SYNTHESIS / REUSE
        |
        +--> candidate common generalizations
        +--> deduplicated lemma structures
        +--> proposed proof refactorings
        +--> measured search-space reduction
```

The engine does not own proof cleanup authority, refereeing authority, canonical theorem interpretation, or exposition authority.

## Useful composition versus mere composition

Future synthesis research must distinguish mechanical compatibility from useful mathematical recombination:

```text
A_TYPECHECKS_WITH_B != USEFUL_SYNTHESIS
COMMON_SUBTRACE != SHARED_MATHEMATICAL_IDEA
SHARED_INVARIANT != THEOREM_EQUIVALENCE
REFACTORING_PROPOSAL != VALID_REFACTORING
SEARCH_REDUCTION != MATHEMATICAL_IMPORTANCE
```

A synthesis candidate is stronger when it prospectively reduces duplicated proof/search effort on held-out cases while preserving independently checked semantics.

## Cleanup interaction

Formal Discovery may propose cleanup candidates such as:

- dead-branch elimination;
- repeated-subproof extraction;
- named lemma candidates;
- dependency simplification;
- proof-structure refactoring;
- candidate assumption minimization.

These remain proposals. Whether a cleaned artifact preserves the theorem belongs to `msk-proof-engineering` and downstream claim governance.

## Digestion interaction

The engine may provide evidence useful to digestion, for example:

- recurrent substructures;
- candidate governing invariants;
- minimal repeated patterns;
- common proof-path motifs;
- observed held-out utility of extracted lemmas.

It must not label a pattern as the canonical mathematical idea merely because it is frequent or operationally useful.

## Prospective metrics

Possible future measurements include:

- held-out proof-search node reduction;
- successful downstream reuse count;
- duplicated subproof eliminated;
- dependency depth reduction;
- candidate lemma reuse across independent problems;
- abstraction stability across representation changes.

These are engineering/scientific observations, not theorem authority.

## Ownership boundary

- `miskatonic-mathematics`: lifecycle semantics, digestion, claim ceilings, canonical interpretation.
- `msk-proof-engineering`: proof custody, refereeing, repair lineage, theorem-preservation checking.
- `msk-formal-discovery`: discovery/search/decomposition/synthesis proposals and prospective utility measurement.
- `msk-onto`: recurrence and invariance studies over provenance-bound structures.

## Activation gate

This seam is parked. It does not alter current Formal Discovery experiments, accepted dispositions, qualification rules, or canonical-library mutation firewalls.

Before activation:

1. a bounded proof-maturation case must be preregistered;
2. original and cleaned/recombined artifacts must remain separately identified;
3. theorem preservation must be checked outside this repository;
4. held-out usefulness must be measured prospectively;
5. no synthesis result may acquire theorem authority from search reduction alone.
